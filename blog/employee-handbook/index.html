What Is an Employee Handbook? (And What to Put in It)

What an employee handbook is, why every business needs one, and a section-by-section list of what to include — from policies to culture.

September 13, 2026 · 6 min read

An employee handbook is the single place your team can go to learn how your company works — the rules, the benefits, the expectations, and the tone. Done well, it prevents a thousand small questions and protects both the business and its people. Done badly, or not at all, it leaves everyone guessing. Here is what a handbook is and what belongs in one.

What is an employee handbook?

An employee handbook is a document that sets out your company's policies, procedures, benefits, and expectations for employees. It is part reference manual, part culture statement: it tells people both what they must do (rules and compliance) and how you do things (values and norms). It is usually given at onboarding and updated as the company grows.

Why every business needs one

A handbook creates consistency — the same rules for everyone — and a written record of the policies you have communicated, which matters if a dispute ever arises. It speeds up onboarding, reduces repetitive questions to HR, and sets expectations clearly so managers are not inventing answers on the spot. Even a small team benefits from writing it down.

What to include

A practical handbook usually covers:

  • Welcome and culture — mission, values, and how you work.
  • Employment basics — contracts, probation, working hours, and attendance.
  • Pay and benefits — payroll schedule, leave, health cover, and any allowances.
  • Leave and time off — annual leave, sick leave, and how to request it.
  • Conduct and expectations — code of conduct, communication, and the disciplinary process.
  • Health, safety, and security — workplace safety and data / IT policies.
  • Anti-discrimination and grievances — how issues are raised and handled.
  • Exit — notice periods and offboarding.

What to leave out

Keep individual contract terms (specific salaries), fast-changing operational detail, and anything better handled case by case out of the handbook — they date quickly and make it heavier than it needs to be. Link to living documents instead of pasting them in.

Keeping it current

A handbook is only useful if it is accurate. Review it at least once a year and whenever the law or a major policy changes, version it so people know what is current, and record that each employee has read and acknowledged it. A stale handbook that contradicts practice is worse than none.

Frequently asked questions

Is an employee handbook legally required?

In most places a handbook itself is not legally required, but many of the individual policies it contains may be — and having them written down helps you stay compliant and consistent. Check the rules where you operate.

What is the difference between an employee handbook and an employment contract?

A contract is a binding agreement with one employee covering their specific terms. A handbook is a general reference of company-wide policies and culture that applies to everyone; it usually is not a contract in itself.

How often should you update an employee handbook?

At least once a year, and immediately whenever a law changes or you introduce or revise a major policy. Version each update and have employees acknowledge the current one.
