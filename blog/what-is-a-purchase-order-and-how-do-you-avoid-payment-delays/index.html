What Is a Purchase Order and How Do You Avoid Payment Delays?

A purchase order is the buyer's written approval to spend. Learn why invoices without a matching PO number stall in accounts payable, and how to prevent it.

October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

You finish the job, send the invoice and wait. Thirty days pass, then forty-five, and the client's accounts payable team replies with one line: “Please resubmit with a valid PO number.” The work was fine and the price was agreed, yet the money is stuck. This guide explains what a purchase order is, why the missing number causes so much delay, and how to stop it happening again.

What is a purchase order?

A purchase order (PO) is a document a buyer issues to a supplier to confirm an order. It lists what is being bought, the quantity, the agreed price and the delivery or payment terms, and it carries a unique PO number. In practice it is the buyer's written approval to spend money: someone with budget authority has agreed to it before any work starts.

For the supplier, a PO is protection. It proves the order was authorised, fixes the price and scope, and gives you a reference that the customer's finance team recognises.

Why do invoices without a matching PO get stuck?

In many companies, accounts payable (AP) does not pay an invoice because it looks correct. It pays because the invoice matches an approved order. When the PO number is missing, wrong or already used up, the invoice cannot be matched automatically and it drops into an exception queue.

From there, a person has to find out who ordered the work, confirm the budget and sometimes raise a PO after the fact. That person is busy, and your invoice is one of hundreds. Common causes include:

  • No PO was issued. The work started on an email or a phone call.
  • The number is mistyped or belongs to a different order.
  • The PO is exhausted. Your invoice exceeds the remaining balance.
  • The details differ. Price, quantity or description does not match the PO.
  • The PO has expired or was closed before you invoiced.

How do you get the PO before work starts?

The cheapest moment to fix a PO problem is before you begin. Make it a standard step in your sales and onboarding process:

  • Ask early. When you send the quote, add a line: “Please send a PO number with your acceptance.”
  • Ask who issues it. Find out whether the requester raises the PO or procurement does, and get a named contact.
  • Make it a start condition. State in your terms that work begins once a valid PO is received.
  • Check the PO on arrival. Confirm the amount, description, dates and billing entity match your quote.
  • Ask about limits. If the work may grow, ask how a PO is increased so you are not stuck halfway.

If a client truly cannot issue a PO in time, get written confirmation from the budget holder and a date by which the PO will follow. Keep that email.

What is 2-way and 3-way matching?

Matching is how AP decides whether an invoice is safe to pay.

2-way match

The invoice is compared with the purchase order. Does the price, quantity and PO number agree? This is common for services and for lower-value purchases.

3-way match

The invoice is compared with the PO and with a goods receipt or delivery confirmation, proof that the items actually arrived or the work was accepted. It is stricter and typical for physical goods and larger spend. If your delivery note is not signed or logged, the third leg is missing and payment waits.

The practical lesson is the same for both: every number on your invoice should be traceable to a document the client already holds.

What should go on the invoice?

  • The PO number, near the top, exactly as the client wrote it, in a clearly labelled field.
  • The legal name and billing address that appear on the PO.
  • Line items that mirror the PO wording, quantities and unit prices.
  • Delivery or receipt reference, such as a delivery note number or the date of acceptance.
  • The total and any tax, with a note if it is a partial or final invoice against the PO.
  • Payment terms and bank details, plus the AP contact the client gave you.

Send one invoice per PO where you can. Mixing several orders on a single invoice makes matching harder and one error can hold the whole amount.

What if the PO is missing? Follow-up steps

  1. Act the day you notice. Do not wait for the due date.
  2. Contact the requester, not only AP. The person who ordered the work can raise the PO fastest. Ask for the number and the expected date.
  3. Put it in writing. Send a short email quoting the invoice number, amount and date, and what you need.
  4. Ask AP what is blocking it. Is it a missing PO, a mismatch or an exhausted balance? Each has a different fix.
  5. Offer to reissue. If the number was wrong, send a corrected invoice or credit note quickly so the clock restarts as soon as possible.
  6. Escalate politely. If there is no answer within a week, copy the budget holder or your account contact.
  7. Record the cause. Note what went wrong so you can fix it at the start of the next job.

Keep the tone helpful. The person in AP is usually following a rule, not refusing to pay.

Make it a habit

PO problems are rarely about the money. They are about process. Ask for the number before you start, check it when it arrives, copy it onto the invoice exactly, and chase early when something is missing. Those four habits remove most of the delay.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a purchase order and an invoice?

A purchase order is issued by the buyer before the work to authorise the purchase. An invoice is issued by the supplier after the work to request payment. The invoice should reference the PO.

Can I get paid without a PO number?

Sometimes, for small or one-off purchases, but many companies will not pay at all until a PO exists. Check your client's policy before starting, and never assume an email is enough.

Who is responsible for raising the PO?

The buyer. Usually the person requesting the work raises it, or procurement does on their behalf. As a supplier, your job is to ask for it early and check it.

What should I do if the PO value is too low?

Contact the requester at once and ask for an amendment or a new PO for the balance. Do not invoice above the PO amount, because AP will probably reject it.

Is a PO a legally binding contract?

Once accepted by the supplier, a PO usually forms a binding agreement on the terms it states. It is still wise to have written terms covering scope, delay and payment.
