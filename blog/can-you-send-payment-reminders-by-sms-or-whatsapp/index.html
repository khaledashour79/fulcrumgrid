Can You Send Invoice Payment Reminders by SMS or WhatsApp?

Yes, if you have consent. Learn the opt-in basics, when a text beats email, ready-to-use message templates, the right tone, tracking and when to escalate.

November 2, 2026 · 6 min read

Email is the default channel for chasing invoices, and it is also the easiest to ignore. Open rates are unpredictable, reminders land in promotions folders, and a late payment can sit unseen for weeks. Text messages and WhatsApp feel more immediate, so it is natural to ask whether you can use them for payment reminders. The short answer is yes, provided you have the right to contact the customer that way and you use the channel with care. This guide covers consent at a high level, when a text beats email, message templates, tone, tracking and escalation.

Do you need consent first?

In most places, yes, or at least you need a clear legal basis. This is a general overview, not legal advice, and rules differ by country, so check the requirements where your customers are based. The principles are broadly consistent:

  • Get opt-in at the start. Ask for a mobile number and permission to send billing messages when you onboard the customer, for example with a tick box on the order form or contract.
  • Say what you will send. Make clear the messages are about invoices and payments, and roughly how often.
  • Offer an easy opt-out. Honour a STOP reply immediately and record it.
  • Respect platform rules. WhatsApp Business requires opt-in, and outside the 24-hour customer-service window you can only send pre-approved message templates.
  • Keep records. Store when and how each customer gave consent, so you can prove it later.
  • Mind the hours. Send during local business hours, never late at night or on public holidays.

Transactional messages about an existing account are often treated more leniently than marketing, but do not assume that. Never mix promotions into a payment reminder.

When does a text beat email?

Text and WhatsApp work best when speed and attention matter more than detail:

  • Short, simple balances. One amount, one due date, one payment link.
  • Individuals and small businesses. Owners who live on their phone rarely check a shared finance inbox.
  • After email has failed. If two emails went unanswered, a message on a different channel breaks the pattern.
  • Time-sensitive nudges. A same-day note on the due date is hard to miss.

Email still wins for formal notices, itemised statements, attachments and anything that needs a paper trail. Think of text as a nudge and email as the record. Use them together rather than choosing one.

Message templates you can adapt

Keep every message under about 300 characters, name your company, state the amount and invoice number, and include one clear action.

Before the due date

  • “Hi Sara, a quick reminder from [Company]: invoice 1042 for $480 is due on 15 Nov. Pay securely here: [link]. Reply STOP to opt out.”

First overdue reminder

  • “Hi Sara, invoice 1042 ($480) was due on 15 Nov and we have not yet seen payment. If it is already on its way, thank you. Otherwise you can pay here: [link].”

Firmer follow-up

  • “Hi Sara, invoice 1042 ($480) is now 14 days overdue. Could you confirm a payment date today? If there is a problem with the invoice, reply and we will sort it out.”

Getting the tone right

A text lands in the same place as messages from friends and family, so it feels personal. Be courteous, brief and human. Assume the invoice was missed, not ignored on purpose. Avoid capital letters, threats, emojis that look flippant and any hint of shaming. Always identify yourself, use the customer's name and offer a way to reply, because a two-way channel is also the fastest way to learn about disputes. Firmness comes from clarity and dates, not from harsh words.

Tracking what works

Treat text reminders like any other step in your collection process. Log every message against the invoice, with the time, channel and template used. Watch delivery status and replies, and record promises to pay with a date. Then compare results: how many invoices were paid within three days of a text versus an email, which template performs best, and which customers never respond on any channel. Without that record you cannot tell whether the channel helps or only feels busy.

Knowing when to escalate

Texts are for early and middle stages. Stop texting when a customer opts out, raises a dispute or agrees a payment plan. If two or three reminders across channels go unanswered, or a promised date passes, move to a phone call, then a formal written notice from a manager, and finally your agreed escalation route. Do not send an endless stream of texts; frequency beyond a sensible cadence feels like harassment and can breach local rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to send payment reminders by SMS?

In most countries yes, if you have the customer's consent or another valid legal basis and you offer an opt-out. Rules vary by country, so check local requirements or ask a lawyer.

Can I use WhatsApp to chase overdue invoices?

Yes, using WhatsApp Business with the customer's opt-in. Outside the 24-hour reply window you can only send pre-approved message templates, so set up templates for reminders in advance.

Should I replace email reminders with texts?

No. Use texts as a short nudge alongside email. Email remains better for formal notices, itemised invoices and a written record.

How many text reminders are too many?

Follow your dunning cadence, typically one message per stage, a week or so apart. Repeated daily messages feel like harassment and may breach local rules.

What should I do if a customer replies STOP?

Stop texting immediately, record the opt-out and switch to another channel such as email or phone for any further collection steps.
