How Do You Get Paid Faster by Large Corporate Customers?

Get paid faster by large companies by finishing vendor onboarding, quoting the right PO, matching AP requirements, negotiating terms and escalating early.

October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Landing a large corporate customer is a milestone, and then the waiting starts. Big companies run payment through approval chains, portals and fixed payment runs, so a perfectly good invoice can sit for months. The good news is that most delays come from avoidable mistakes. This guide covers the steps that move you up the queue.

Why do large companies pay so slowly?

It is rarely personal. Accounts payable (AP) teams process thousands of invoices, and they follow rules built to prevent fraud and duplicate payments. An invoice is usually checked against a purchase order (PO) and a receipt of goods or services before it is approved. If anything fails to match, the invoice is parked in an exception queue, and nobody tells you. Many large buyers also pay on a fixed cycle, such as weekly or twice a month, so missing a cut-off date can cost you two extra weeks.

What should you finish before the first invoice?

Start with vendor onboarding. Most corporates will not pay a supplier that is not fully set up in their system. Expect to provide:

  • Tax forms and registration numbers
  • Bank details, often verified by a call-back or a document
  • Insurance certificates and compliance or security questionnaires
  • A named contact for invoice questions

Ask your sponsor for the onboarding status in writing and chase it before work begins. Then confirm how the customer wants to receive invoices, because many reject anything sent to a personal inbox.

Why do purchase orders matter so much?

Many large buyers follow a “no PO, no pay” policy. Get the PO number before you start, check that the amount and description cover the work, and ask for an updated PO if the scope changes. An invoice that exceeds the PO balance is one of the most common reasons for rejection.

How do you get through AP portals and invoice requirements?

Many corporates require invoices through a supplier portal or a dedicated AP email address. Submit there, not to your project contact. Then check your invoice against the buyer’s checklist. Typical requirements include:

  • The exact legal entity name and billing address
  • The PO number on the invoice, in the field the portal expects
  • Line items that mirror the PO or contract
  • Correct tax or VAT details and a unique invoice number
  • Supporting documents, such as timesheets or a delivery note

Keep a copy of the portal confirmation. If the system shows a status like “received” or “approved for payment,” note the date, since that is what you will quote later.

What can you do about net-60 and net-90 terms?

Long terms are often non-negotiable on paper, but you still have options. Ask for shorter terms when you sign, or in exchange for volume or a longer commitment. Offer an early-payment discount, for example 1% to 2% for payment within 10 days, and compare it with your cost of borrowing. Invoice at milestones or monthly rather than at the end of a project, so cash arrives in smaller pieces. Some buyers also run supply chain finance programs that let you be paid early for a fee, so ask whether one exists. Whatever you agree, get it into the contract, and check whether the clock starts on the invoice date or on the date the invoice is approved.

When and how should you escalate to AP?

Do not wait until the invoice is overdue. A simple rhythm works well:

  • On submission: confirm the invoice was received and ask whether anything is missing.
  • A week before the due date: check the approval status.
  • On the due date: ask for the scheduled payment date.
  • After that: contact the AP specialist, then their manager, and ask your business sponsor to help.

Keep messages short and factual: invoice number, PO number, amount, due date and the one thing you need. Polite, specific and consistent beats loud.

Does e-invoicing help?

Yes. Structured e-invoices, delivered through a network or portal, are matched automatically, which cuts manual errors and lost documents. Several countries already require them for businesses or public bodies, and more are moving that way, so check the rules where you and your customer operate. If your buyer supports e-invoicing, use it.

What should you do next?

Pick your slowest large customer and audit one recent invoice against the list above. Fix the cause of any delay in the process, not just in that invoice. Frequently asked questions

What is a normal payment term for large corporate customers?

Net-30 to net-90 is common, and net-60 is typical for large buyers. Check the contract, and confirm whether the clock starts on the invoice date or the approval date.

Why was my invoice rejected without any explanation?

Usually a mismatch: a wrong or missing PO number, an amount above the PO balance, or the wrong legal entity. Ask the AP contact for the exception reason and resubmit.

Who should I contact when a corporate invoice is overdue?

Start with the AP specialist named in the portal or the vendor setup, then their manager. Also ask your business sponsor, who can approve or unblock the invoice.

Can I charge late fees to large customers?

Only if the contract or local law allows it. Many large buyers ignore late fees that are not agreed in advance, so negotiate the clause at signing.

Is e-invoicing mandatory?

It depends on the country and the type of customer. Some regions require it for public bodies or for all businesses, so check local rules and your buyer&rsquo;s requirements.
