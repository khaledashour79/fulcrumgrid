What Is E-Invoicing and Will It Help You Get Paid Faster?

E-invoicing swaps PDF invoices for structured data your customer's system can read. Learn how it works, where it speeds up payment and where it does not.

November 7, 2026 · 6 min read

Invoices used to travel by post, then by email as PDFs. Now a growing number of countries are asking businesses to send them as structured data instead. If you issue invoices, you have probably heard the term e-invoicing and wondered whether it is just extra paperwork or something that improves your cash flow. This guide answers the common questions honestly, including what e-invoicing cannot do.

What is e-invoicing?

E-invoicing is the exchange of invoices in a structured, machine-readable format, such as XML, directly between the seller's system and the buyer's system. The details (supplier, amounts, tax, due date, line items) sit in defined fields, so software can read them without anyone retyping a thing.

Is a PDF invoice an e-invoice?

Usually not. A PDF sent by email is electronic, but it is only an image of a document. A person still has to read it and key the details into an accounting system. A true e-invoice can be processed automatically. Some formats combine both, a readable PDF with structured data embedded, which is a useful halfway step.

How does e-invoicing work?

  • Create. Your accounting or invoicing software generates the invoice in the required format.
  • Send. It travels through a network, a service provider or a government portal, depending on the country.
  • Validate. The format and key fields are checked, so errors are caught early.
  • Receive and process. The customer's system reads the data, matches it to the purchase order and routes it for approval.

Will e-invoicing help you get paid faster?

Often, but not automatically. E-invoicing speeds up everything that happens before the customer decides to pay. It does not change the payment terms you agreed or how much cash your customer has. Picture a PDF invoice sent on the 1st, opened on the 4th, retyped on the 6th and queried on the 9th because the purchase order number is missing. Nine days have passed and nobody has started approving it. An e-invoice that matches the purchase order on arrival can reach the approver the same day.

Faster delivery

The invoice lands in the customer's system within minutes, with no lost emails, no spam folders and no postal delay.

Fewer errors

Validation catches missing tax numbers, wrong totals and incomplete fields before you send. Many late payments start with an invoice that was rejected or queried.

Quicker approval

When the data matches the purchase order automatically, the invoice can move through approval without manual entry, which is where invoices often sit for days.

Clearer status

Some networks show whether an invoice was delivered, accepted or rejected, so you stop guessing whether it arrived.

What e-invoicing will not fix

  • Long payment terms. A 60-day term is still 60 days.
  • Disputes. If the customer disagrees with the work or the amount, no file format will settle it.
  • Customers short of cash. Delivering an invoice faster does not create the money to pay it.
  • Missing follow-up. Someone still has to notice when an invoice becomes overdue and ask about it.

Is e-invoicing mandatory?

It depends on where you and your customers are. A growing number of countries require e-invoicing for sales to public bodies, and some extend it to business-to-business invoices, often in phases by company size. Rules, formats and deadlines differ widely, so check with your tax authority or accountant rather than relying on a general guide.

How to get the most out of it

  • Clean your customer data. Correct legal names, tax numbers and references prevent rejections.
  • Include the purchase order number. Many customers cannot approve an invoice without it.
  • State the due date clearly and keep payment terms short where you can.
  • Agree the channel in advance. Ask large customers how they prefer to receive invoices.
  • Track every invoice through to payment. Sending is only half the job.

E-invoicing removes friction at the start of the payment journey. Getting paid on time still depends on clear terms, accurate invoices and consistent follow-up.

Frequently asked questions

Is e-invoicing the same as sending an invoice by email?

No. An emailed PDF is only a picture of an invoice that someone must read and retype. An e-invoice carries structured data that the receiving system can process automatically.

Does e-invoicing guarantee faster payment?

No. It removes delays in delivery, data entry and approval, but payment terms, disputes and your customer's cash position still decide when you are paid.

Do small businesses have to use e-invoicing?

It depends on the country and the type of customer. Some rules apply only to sales to public bodies or to larger companies at first. Check with your tax authority or accountant.

Do I need new software to start?

Often you do not need a separate tool, because many accounting packages can already produce e-invoices or connect to a network. Ask your software provider what is supported in your country.
