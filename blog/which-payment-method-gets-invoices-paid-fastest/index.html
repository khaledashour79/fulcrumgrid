Which Payment Method Gets Invoices Paid Fastest: ACH, Wire, Card or Check?

Card and wire usually land fastest, ACH is the best balance of cost and speed, and checks are slowest. Compare speed, fees, risk and how to steer customers.

October 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Sending an invoice is only half the job. How the customer pays decides whether the money arrives today, next week or after a trip to the bank. This guide answers the questions small businesses ask most about which payment method gets invoices paid fastest, what each one costs, and how to nudge customers toward the better options.

Which payment method is fastest?

For the time between “customer decides to pay” and “money is usable”, the usual ranking is: wire and card first, ACH next, check last. But the real question is which method gets customers to pay sooner. A card link that takes 30 seconds often beats a wire that the customer has to look up bank details for. Speed is a mix of settlement time and how easy the method is to start.

How do the four methods compare?

ACH (bank transfer)

ACH is the low-cost workhorse. Standard transfers typically settle in one to three business days, and same-day options exist at many banks. Fees are usually small, often a flat amount of well under two dollars or a low percentage with a cap. The catch is that a payment can be returned, for example for insufficient funds, so treat it as paid only once it has cleared.

Wire transfer

Wires are fast, often the same day, and hard to reverse, which makes them popular for large or international payments. They are also the most expensive per transaction, commonly a fixed fee of roughly 15 to 30 dollars charged by one or both banks. The customer must enter your account details correctly, so errors and delays from typing mistakes are common.

Credit or debit card

Cards are the easiest for the customer and usually the quickest to start: click, enter details, done. Funds typically reach your account within one to three business days. The cost is the highest in percentage terms, often around 3% plus a small fixed fee, and cards carry the most dispute risk.

Paper check

Checks are the slowest and least predictable. Add printing, mailing time, a trip to the bank or a mobile deposit, and a possible hold, and ten days or more is normal. They are cheap in fees but expensive in staff time, and a check can still bounce days after you thought it was paid.

What about cost and failure risk?

  • ACH: low fees; returns possible, mostly within a few banking days, and longer for unauthorized debits.
  • Wire: high fees; very low reversal risk once sent; wrong-account errors are painful to fix.
  • Card: highest fees; chargebacks can arrive weeks or even months later, and you pay fees on disputed amounts.
  • Check: lowest fees; bounced checks, lost mail and slow deposits.

A simple rule: use ACH as your default, wire for large or cross-border invoices, and card for small amounts or customers who value convenience.

How do you nudge customers toward faster methods?

  • Put a pay link on the invoice. A visible “Pay now” button removes the biggest delay: finding out how to pay.
  • List the preferred method first. Show ACH details or a bank-pay link above everything else, and mention checks last or not at all.
  • Use direct wording. “Fastest way to pay: bank transfer via the link below. Funds arrive in 1–2 business days.” works better than a generic payment-terms paragraph.
  • Offer a small early-payment discount. Terms such as 2% off if paid within 10 days reward speed without penalizing anyone.
  • Offer autopay for repeat clients. Saved bank details or a card on file remove the decision entirely.
  • Make the wire details copy-ready. Include account name, number, bank code and a payment reference that matches the invoice number.
  • Add a reminder. A friendly message with the same pay link a few days before the due date gets many invoices paid on time.

Can you charge a surcharge for card payments?

Sometimes, but the rules vary a lot. Some places allow card surcharges within limits, some ban them outright, and some apply them only to certain card types. Card networks and your payment provider also have their own conditions, usually including a cap and clear disclosure. A safer approach in many cases is to offer a discount for paying by bank transfer instead of adding a card fee, though even that should be checked. Before you add any surcharge, check your local law, your card agreement and your provider’s terms, show the amount clearly before payment, and consider asking a legal or accounting adviser. This article is general information, not legal advice.

What should you do next?

Pick one default method, put its link on every invoice, and review your average days-to-pay after a month. If card payments are eating your margin or checks are slowing cash flow, change the order in which you present options, not the customer’s freedom to choose. Small wording and layout changes often move more money than any fee comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Is ACH or wire faster for paying an invoice?

Wire is usually faster, often the same day, but ACH is cheaper and one to three business days is acceptable for most invoices. Choose wire for large or urgent payments.

Which payment method has the lowest fees?

Checks and ACH usually cost the least in fees. Cards cost the most, commonly around 3% plus a fixed fee, and wires carry a flat fee per transfer.

Can an ACH payment be reversed?

Yes. ACH payments can be returned, for example for insufficient funds or an unauthorized debit, so consider the invoice settled only after the funds have cleared.

Is it legal to add a surcharge for card payments?

It depends on where you and your customer are. Some regions allow capped surcharges, others ban them, and card networks add rules. Check local law and your provider's terms first.

How can I get customers to stop paying by check?

Put a pay link and bank details on the invoice, list checks last, and offer a small early-payment discount or autopay for repeat clients.
