What Is a North Star Metric (and How Do You Choose One)?

A north star metric is the one number that captures the value your product delivers. Learn what it is, why it matters, and how to pick the right one.

September 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Ask ten founders what their single most important metric is and you'll often get ten answers — sometimes ten from the same founder on ten different days. A north star metric fixes that. It is the one number that best captures the value your product delivers to customers, and it becomes the thing your whole team steers by. This guide explains what a north star metric is, why it matters, how it differs from vanity metrics and KPIs, and how to choose one that actually moves your business.

What is a north star metric?

A north star metric is the single measure that most closely reflects the core value customers get from your product. If that number climbs over time, it should mean customers are getting more of what they came for — and that durable growth follows. Spotify famously watches time spent listening; Airbnb watches nights booked; a B2B tool might watch weekly active teams. The point is not the specific number but the discipline of choosing one and rallying around it.

Why does it matter?

Most teams drown in dashboards. Marketing optimizes signups, sales optimizes deals, product optimizes features — and nobody is sure the sum adds up to a healthier business. A north star metric gives everyone a shared definition of progress. It aligns priorities, makes trade-offs easier (“does this move the north star?”), and keeps you honest about whether growth is real or borrowed.

North star vs. vanity metrics vs. KPIs

A vanity metric looks good on a slide but doesn't reflect real value or predict revenue — total registered users, page views, app downloads, social followers. It can rise while the business quietly stalls.

A KPI is any key performance indicator you track to monitor the health of a function: churn, gross margin, on-time delivery, response time. You have many KPIs.

A north star metric sits above the KPIs. It is the one company-level measure that best connects customer value to growth. The KPIs are the supporting cast; the north star is the lead.

What makes a good north star metric?

  • It reflects customer value. When it grows, customers are genuinely better off, not just counted.
  • It is leading, not lagging. Revenue is a lagging result; a good north star — weekly active teams, orders fulfilled — tends to predict revenue before it shows up.
  • It is a single number. If you need three metrics to explain your progress, you haven't chosen yet.
  • Your team can influence it. People should be able to draw a line from their daily work to the metric.
  • It is hard to game. You can't inflate it without also delivering real value.

Concrete examples across business types

  • Marketplace: completed transactions per week — it captures supply and demand actually meeting.
  • SaaS / subscription: weekly active accounts, or “activated” customers who reach a key action — usage predicts renewal.
  • E-commerce: repeat purchase rate or orders per active customer — one-time buyers rarely build a business.
  • Services / agency: projects delivered on time, or retained clients — value shows up in delivery, not proposals.

Notice that none of these is simply “revenue.” Revenue is the reward; the north star is the leading behavior that earns it.

How to cascade it into team KPIs

A north star is useless if it lives only in a board deck. Break it into the inputs each team owns. If your north star is weekly active teams, product might own activation rate, customer success might own retention of active teams, marketing might own qualified signups, and sales might own seats sold. Each team's KPI is a lever on the same number. Review them together, weekly, so it's obvious when one lever is dragging the rest.

Common pitfalls

  • Choosing revenue itself. It's lagging, and it hides why growth is happening.
  • Picking a vanity metric. Total signups feels great and teaches you nothing.
  • Changing it every quarter. A north star only works if it's stable enough to steer by.
  • Optimizing it in isolation. Watch guardrail metrics — churn, margin, satisfaction — so you don't juice the north star at the company's expense.

Choose deliberately, keep it stable, and connect every team's work to it. A good north star metric won't run your business for you — but it will tell you, faster than anything else, whether you're winning.

Frequently asked questions

Can a company have more than one north star metric?

Ideally no. The whole value is forcing a single, shared definition of progress. Large companies sometimes give each product line its own north star, but within one team, one number keeps everyone aligned.

Is revenue a good north star metric?

Usually not. Revenue is a lagging outcome &mdash; it tells you what already happened, not what's driving it. A better north star is the leading customer behavior, like active usage or repeat purchases, that produces the revenue.

How is a north star metric different from a KPI?

A KPI monitors the health of one function, and you track many of them. A north star is the single company-level metric that best links customer value to growth. Your KPIs support the north star.

How often should we change our north star metric?

Rarely. It should be stable enough to steer by for years. Revisit it only when your product or business model fundamentally changes; changing it frequently defeats its purpose.
