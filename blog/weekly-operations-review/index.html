How to Run a Weekly Operations Review (Agenda + Template)

A practical guide to running a weekly operations review — a repeatable agenda, the metrics to open with, and how to keep the meeting short and decision-focused.

August 31, 2026 · 6 min read

A weekly operations review is the meeting that keeps a business honest. Done well, it catches problems while they're still small, keeps everyone pointed at the same priorities, and turns "how are we doing?" into a five-minute glance instead of a week of guessing. Done badly, it's a status meeting nobody prepares for and nobody remembers. The difference is almost entirely structure.

Here's a repeatable format you can run in 30–45 minutes, plus the agenda to copy.

Before the meeting: the numbers are already in

The single biggest time-saver is that no one should be pulling numbers during the review. The metrics that open the meeting — this week vs. last week, vs. target — should be ready before anyone sits down. If half the meeting is spent asking "what's the latest figure?", the review has already failed. A live dashboard everyone can see beforehand fixes this.

The agenda

  1. Metrics scan (5 min). Walk the handful of numbers that define the business this week — revenue or sales, cash and receivables, delivery or output, and one or two health signals. Green stays quiet; anything off-track gets a flag, not a discussion, yet.
  2. Flags and exceptions (15 min). Take each flagged item in turn. What changed, what's the cause, what's the decision or owner. This is where the meeting earns its place — keep it to things that are actually off-track.
  3. Last week's actions (5 min). Quick pass on the commitments from last time: done, in progress, or dropped. Accountability compounds when it's checked every week.
  4. This week's priorities (5 min). Agree the two or three things that matter most, with a name against each.
  5. Close (2 min). Confirm the action list and owners. Everyone should leave knowing exactly what they own.

Rules that keep it short

  • Same agenda, every week. Predictability is what lets people prepare and keeps the meeting fast.
  • Data on screen, not in a deck. A live view beats slides someone rebuilt by hand — and it can't be quietly massaged.
  • Discuss exceptions, not everything. If a metric is on track, it gets seconds, not minutes.
  • Every issue leaves with an owner. A problem with no name against it will be back next week, unchanged.
  • Protect the time box. Deep dives get scheduled separately, not run live while everyone waits.

What to actually put on the dashboard

Keep it to the numbers that drive decisions — not every metric you can measure. If you're deciding what belongs, our guide to what to track in an operations dashboard covers the signal-versus-noise call. And if those numbers still live across a stack of spreadsheets, moving off spreadsheets is what makes a single, trustworthy view possible in the first place.

The review is only as good as the data behind it. When the metrics are scattered, half the meeting is reconciliation; when they live in one place and update themselves, the review becomes what it should be — a fast, honest look at reality and a short list of decisions.
